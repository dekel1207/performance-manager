## Engineering Manager

##### Key characteristics:

###### Meta

- Engineering Managers own their team's total output and ensure it delivers significant value.
- At this level, managers focus on building competency in core management skills.

###### Technology

- Personally contribute to the stack through strategic contributions and as a valuable code and architectural reviewer.
- Participate in the team's on-call and ensure sustainability of the rotation.
- Are accountable for the quality of the team's technical output.

##### Process

- Partner to create, articulate, and execute towards both technical and product vision for their team on a 6+ month horizon.
- Serve as effective project managers, creating and running effective processes for the team (standups, sprint planning, technical design).

##### People

- Manage all aspects of performance, including: growth pathing, feedback, performance management. Through a combination of effective hiring, performance management, and coaching, an EM is accountable for ensuring that their direct reports have increasingly high levels of impact over time.
- Hire and on-board new team members adeptly.
- Create a fulfilling work environment for the team's engineers.
- Drive clarity when ambiguity blocks progress. Effectively communicate to ensure any dependencies or coordination are managed effectively.
- Act to create clarity in ambiguous situations, inform others of necessary information, and in collaboration with cross-functional team members, leadership, and peers.

## Senior Engineering Manager

##### Key Characteristics:

##### Meta

- Unless otherwise stated, all of the expectations of an Engineering Manager exist in the Senior Engineering Manager role.
- Senior Engineering Managers manage an increased level of complexity and focus on applying management principles to a breadth of projects and teams.
- Lead at least one workstream beyond the day-to-day operations of a single team.
- Own a track record of clearly identifiable impact on their division's operations.
- Operate teams at a high level of delivery, providing high impact during long-term (1 year or more) projects.

##### Technology

- Be a technical leader through goal setting and via architecture and design review.
- Facilitate, ideate, and drive multi-quarter engineering strategy and initiatives, leading them to impactful results.
- Senior Manager technical contributions should scale inversely with their team size.

##### Process

- Partner to create, articulate, and execute towards both technical and product vision for their team(s) on a 1+ year horizon.
- Operate autonomously across complex environments and create effective plans out of undefined problems.
- Design and support larger-scale process improvements.

##### People

- Develop technical leadership within their group, maintaining technical direction without having to be an expert on all details.
- Drive recruiting strategy and create differentiated hiring outcomes for their teams.
- Create a fulfilling work environment across their group.
- Serve as agents of stability and productive collaboration across their team(s), cross-functional team members, and leadership.

## Calibration guidance

### EM reviews

Use the **EM bar only**. Evaluate each dimension against the EM ladder expectations. A score-3 EM meets most dimensions consistently; score-4 exceeds several and shows early SEM behaviors.

### SEM reviews

Apply the **EM bar per team** first (is each team being managed to EM standard?), then layer the **SEM bar** on top (are they operating at the SEM level across teams?).

### Score descriptors

| Score | Label | What it means |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| 4 | Exceptional | Consistently exceeds bar; rare behaviors at the next level |
| 3–4 | Strong 3 leaning 4 | Meets bar solidly; shows some next-level behaviors |
| 3 | Meets expectations | Meets bar; no significant gaps |
| 2–3 | Weak 3 | Generally meets bar with notable gaps in 1–2 areas |
| 2 | Below expectations | Significant gaps; needs a plan to address them |
| 1 | Not meeting bar | Fundamental gaps; performance-managed or role mismatch |
